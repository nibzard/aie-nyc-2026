## Key lessons
1. The AI engineer role is shifting toward agent management. Day two partner Thinakaran of Arize asked "What does an AI engineer do?" and day three answers with Ryan Carson's session "Your Job Is Now Agent Manager." Kseniya quips that the job description "just changed overnight" and hopes compensation keeps up.
2. The sponsor roster maps the ecosystem: Arize is the presenting sponsor; Oracle, Sonar, WorkOS, and Axa are platinum; gold, silver, and supporting partners complete the list. She notes that several sponsors also employ the attendees, so they see the responsibility shift firsthand.
3. The three days form a progression. Monday held hands-on workshops, from Cerebras and OpenAI to the startup OpenPros. Tuesday covered how AI translates into operating margin. Day three covers the capabilities companies build toward, the work agents can take on, and what managing that involves.
4. Day three content splits by room. Shannon Room: finance, compute, and infrastructure. Turing Room: evaluations, benchmarking, retrieval, self-improving agents, and skills. Sutton Room: developer tools, enterprise data, software factories, agent permissions, and token economics. McCarthy Room: model costs, coding agent context, human verification, and banking deployment with financial agent evidence.
5. Turing Post's "Org Age of AI" series frames three questions for companies adopting the technology: how the work changes, what becomes practical that was not before, and why ROI still lags while superintelligence feels powerful.
6. The event's value comes from mixing the two groups: people building the technology and people who understand the work in detail. The first speaker, Ramp, works at the intersection of those worlds.
