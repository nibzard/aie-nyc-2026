# Welcome Remarks

> MC Alex Lieberman opens AI Engineer New York 2026 by calling the room an "Edge City" that lives six months to two years ahead of AI consensus, frames the event as "AI engineering meets Wall Street," thanks the AIE team and sponsors, and hands off to the first keynote from Bridgewater.

## TL;DR
- Alex Lieberman introduces himself as co-founder and co-managing partner at 10X, an applied AI company that helps Fortune 500 businesses become AI-native, and as prior co-founder of Morning Brew.
- He frames X (formerly Twitter) as a place to "live in the future," then calls this conference "X on steroids."
- He introduces the "Edge City" concept: somewhere in the world, a group of people always lives in the future of a specialty. This conference is Edge City for AI in finance, six months to two years ahead of consensus.
- He positions the event: AI Engineer New York 2026, "where AI engineering meets Wall Street," for engineers and technical leaders shipping production AI in banking, hedge funds, fintech, insurance, and asset management.
- He thanks the AIE team and the sponsor tiers: presenting sponsor Arize; platinum sponsors Oracle, Sonar, WorkOS, and Exa; then gold, silver, and supporting sponsors.
- He closes by introducing the first keynote: "two amazing folks from Bridgewater" who work in production AI every day.

## Key lessons
1. The "Edge City" framing gives a useful mental model for conferences: at any point, some group of people somewhere lives in the future of a specific function or specialty. Lieberman argues the attendees are that group for AI diffusion, six months to two years ahead of consensus.
2. Lieberman treats X (formerly Twitter) as a leading indicator. Scrolling commentary there feels like seeing what becomes consensus in three, six, or 12 months. He says that is exactly why the conference feels like "X on steroids," but in person.
3. The conference's stated identity is precise: "AI engineering meets Wall Street." The audience is engineers and technical leaders building production AI across banking, hedge funds, fintech, insurance, and asset management.
4. The two-day program covers two layers: the technology stack (models, agents, infra, compute, dev tools, evals) and how that stack reshapes the financial industry, including hedge funds, investors, banks, insurance, fintech, and security.
5. The MC sets a tone pattern worth noting: self-introduction with credentials (10X co-founder, Morning Brew co-founder), an audience poll as an icebreaker, a big idea, then logistics. The poll asked who is "healthily addicted to X"; his follow-up joke was "a lot of people are lying in the room."
6. Sponsor acknowledgment ran by tier: presenting sponsor Arize; platinum sponsors Oracle, Sonar, WorkOS, and Exa; followed by gold, silver, and supporting sponsors. The AIE team got thanks before the sponsors.
7. The first keynote was introduced with a credibility hook rather than a name: "two amazing folks from Bridgewater who are in the weeds of production AI every single day."

## Session narrative
Alex Lieberman opened with a casual greeting and a quick self-introduction. He named himself as co-founder and co-managing partner at 10X, an applied AI company that works with Fortune 500 businesses to become AI-native. He added his prior credential: co-founder of Morning Brew, "which makes business news suck a little bit less." He then ran an icebreaker poll, asking for raised hands from anyone "healthily addicted to X, or formerly known as Twitter," and joked that many non-raised hands were lies.

The core idea came next. Lieberman explained why he spends so much time on X: the commentary feels like a preview of what becomes consensus in three, six, or 12 months. A friend once described "edge city" to him: at any point, somewhere in the world, a group of people lives in the future of a specific function or specialty. He mapped that concept onto the room. This conference, he argued, is Edge City for AI in finance. Everyone present lives six months to two years ahead of where the technology's diffusion will land.

He then made the conference framing official. Welcome to AI Engineer New York 2026, "where AI engineering meets Wall Street." The event targets engineers and technical leaders who build production AI across banking, hedge funds, fintech, insurance, and asset management. Over two days, speakers would cover models, agents, infra, compute, dev tools, and evals, and how those weave into the financial industry.

He closed with gratitude and a hand-off. First came a shout out to the AIE team for assembling the event. Then sponsor thanks by tier: presenting sponsor Arize; platinum sponsors Oracle, Sonar, WorkOS, and Exa; then gold, silver, and supporting sponsors, which he had no time to rattle off one by one. Finally, he introduced the first keynote of the day: two speakers from Bridgewater who are "in the weeds of production AI every single day," and welcomed them to the stage.

## Speaker details
**Alex Lieberman** - Managing Partner, Tenex. Serial entrepreneur best known as co-founder of Morning Brew, the business media company he started as a college newsletter and grew to 2M+ daily readers before its ~$75M sale to Business Insider in 2020. He is now co-founder and Managing Partner of Tenex, an AI engineering and transformation firm that helps companies go from AI-absent to AI-native. He also founded Storyarb and Distro, hosts the Founder's Journal podcast, and co-hosts The Crazy Ones. Recognized on Forbes 30 Under 30 and AdAge 40 Under 40; based in New York City. At AI Engineer NYC 2026 he served as MC.
