This unofficial site covers two main-stage livestreams from AI Engineer NYC 2026:
October 13 and October 14. Day 1 and Day 2 here label those two recording dates.
The coverage is the Simons Room feed, including recorded talks played during breaks.
It does not include every talk in the other rooms.

The [engineering field guide](/) compares approaches across both days. Each topic guide explains the evidence limits and proposes a small experiment. Each
[session summary](/sessions-index/) includes key lessons and a timestamped transcript.
Use the day filter to narrow the list, or start with the [Day 1 digest](/day1/)
and [Day 2 digest](/day2/).

## How to use the notes

Start with a topic on the home page to compare approaches across days.
Open a session for our notes and lessons. Use its source link when you need
more context or want to check a claim. Matching Day 1 sessions link to the
[official reading editions](https://aie-nyc-2026-talks.swyxio.workers.dev/), which include slides and searchable transcripts.

Numbers and results are the speakers’ reported findings. Some describe one experiment,
a specific workload, or a future plan. The session notes preserve those limits.
The field guide is our editorial synthesis of the two recordings. Comparisons and
suggested experiments are our interpretation, rather than procedures prescribed by the speakers.

We checked speaker attribution across the official October 13 library and reviewed
selected numerical claims. This corrected the Railway and Flywire speaker names.
Vals’ $1.5 million token figure describes the reported value of free access. Railway’s
“700 million tokens” line is an aside. GPU security results describe separate experiments,
not a general success rate. The original machine transcript text remains available in the session notes.

The Day 2 catalog contains two records of the same Intuit Credit Karma talk.
The session browser lists that talk once. Both source records remain available.
Announcements, breaks, and alternate records stay in the stream archives.

## How timestamps work

Each recording starts its video clock at zero. A timestamp includes the video
position and the wall-clock time, for example `[01:23:45 | 10:11:37 AM ET]`.
ET means Eastern Time. Each video link opens the correct day’s recording at the
specified position.

## How the notes were produced

1. Download the source audio and split it into chunks.
2. Transcribe the chunks and align the text with timestamps.
3. Map the transcript to the event program and verify the session boundaries.
4. Write session notes, enrich speaker records, and audit the references.
5. Synthesize lessons across both days and link each lesson to its source sessions.
