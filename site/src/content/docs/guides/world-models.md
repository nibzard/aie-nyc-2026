**Do you need a model that selects actions or generates an environment?**

Compare General Intuition’s action-labeled game data with Runway’s video simulation approach. Keep shipped demonstrations separate from future promises.

[All topic guides](/#find-a-lesson-for-what-you-are-building) · [Practical takeaways](/#practical-takeaways-for-ai-engineers)

## Where the approaches meet

Both talks explore learning from visual experience. Their demonstrations suggest possibilities beyond text, while leaving important deployment questions open.

## How the approaches differ

| Session | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| General Intuition · Day 1 | Uses action-labeled game data to train models that continuously observe and select actions. |
| Runway · Day 2 | Uses next-frame prediction to generate visual environments and interfaces. Its Continuum demonstration explores a video-based operating system. |

[General Intuition · Day 1](/sessions/046-frontier-models-for-acting-in-space-and-time-pixels-to-actio/) · [Runway · Day 2](/day2/049-everything-will-be-simulated-runway-video-models-next-frame/)

## Where the evidence stops

Action selection and environment generation are different tasks. A convincing video does not establish reliable physical behavior. Runway’s release window is a speaker’s plan, and some demonstrations were not generated in real time.

## Try this on your workload

1. Write down whether your use case needs action selection, visual prediction, or both.
2. Choose a small recorded interaction and define an observable success condition.
3. Test a repeatable baseline or an available model on held-out interactions before designing around a demonstrated research feature.

### Measure the result

Track task success, response latency, repeatability, and failures when the environment changes. Report unavailable capabilities as requirements still to test.
