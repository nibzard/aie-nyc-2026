MC Alex Lieberman returns after the afternoon break to thank the sponsor tiers, argues the conference content is "surface area" for hallway conversations, shares Tenex's enterprise AI diffusion ladder (90% LLM rollout, 60% trained users, 50% coding agents, 5% non-engineering agents), and introduces Frank Long of Coatue for "A Hedge Fund as a Function."

## TL;DR
- Lieberman resumes mid-thought ("For Fortune 500 businesses") after a roughly 10-minute untranscribed gap, checks in on the day, and thanks the sponsors by tier: presenting sponsor Arize; platinum sponsors Oracle, Sonar, WorkOS, and Exa; then gold, silver, and supporting partners.
- He tells the room the talks themselves are just the starting point: the content, speakers, and depth are "surface area" for the conversations attendees have with each other.
- Recounting a backstage conversation with Swix, he shares Tenex's enterprise AI diffusion ladder from roughly 100 enterprise clients: 90% have rolled out a general-purpose LLM to all employees, about 60% have people actually trained on the tools, around 50% use coding agents in engineering and product workflows, and only about 5% have agents working in non-engineering workflows.
- He runs the first stat as an interactive audience poll ("Someone scream out a number") and gets guesses of 9%, 17%, and 90% before revealing the answer.
- He names the day's recurring hallway themes: the diffusion gap, open source versus fine-tuned models, and how hard it is to hire great AI engineers at scale.
- He closes the block by introducing Frank Long, head of AI at Coatue (rendered "Kotu" in the transcript), for the talk "A Hedge Fund as a Function."

## Key lessons
1. Tenex's diffusion ladder for enterprise AI, from about 100 enterprise conversations across financial services, healthcare, and industrials: 90% have adopted and rolled out a general-purpose LLM to all employees, but only about 60% have employees actually trained in using the technology, about 50% use coding agents in engineering and product workflows, and roughly 5% have agents working in non-engineering workflows.
2. Lieberman's takeaway from the ladder: enterprises are "truly still day one" on the hardest milestone — agents in non-engineering workflows — which is the gap this community's topics will fill over the next 12, 24, and 36 months.
3. His conference method: treat talks as surface area, not endpoints. Use each session as a prompt for conversations "at the edge" with other attendees, which is where the value of an in-person event lives.
4. Interactive stat delivery works on a live stage: he asks the room to scream out a percentage before revealing the 90% LLM-rollout figure, turning a data point into audience engagement.
5. The sponsor structure of AI Engineer NYC 2026 day one: presenting sponsor Arize; platinum sponsors Oracle, Sonar, WorkOS, and Exa; plus gold sponsors ("They're all my favorite children"), silver sponsors, and supporting partners.
6. The hallway track's dominant themes on day one, per Lieberman: the enterprise diffusion gap, open source versus fine-tuned models, and the difficulty of hiring great AI engineers at scale.

## Speaker details
**Alex Lieberman** - Managing Partner, Tenex. Serial entrepreneur best known as co-founder of Morning Brew, the business media company he started as a college newsletter and grew to 2M+ daily readers before its ~$75M sale to Business Insider in 2020. He is now co-founder and Managing Partner of Tenex, an AI engineering and transformation firm that helps companies go from AI-absent to AI-native. He also founded Storyarb and Distro, hosts the Founder's Journal podcast, and co-hosts The Crazy Ones.
